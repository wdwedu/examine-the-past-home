Era 01

Deep Past

Human origins in Africa, migration, adaptation, climate, tools, symbolic culture, and the long path toward settled communities.
c. 3.2 million years ago–10,000 BCE
🧬
c. 300,000 years agoHumanity

Early Homo sapiens

Fossil evidence places early members of our species in Africa hundreds of thousands of years ago.

🔥
Deep prehistoryScience & Technology

Fire, Tools & Adaptation

Controlled fire and increasingly specialized tools transformed food preparation, protection, and social life.

🦴
c. 117,000–108,000 years agoHumanity

Last Known Homo erectus

New dating of fossils from Java indicates Homo erectus survived there until roughly 108,000–117,000 years ago.

🪨
c. 12,000–10,000 BCESociety

Settled Communities Expand

In several regions, people increasingly combined hunting and gathering with more settled lifeways and food production.

🦴
c. 3.2 million years agoHumanity

Lucy and Australopithecus afarensis

The fossil skeleton known as Lucy became one of the most famous windows into early human evolution.

🧬
c. 315,000 years agoHumanity

Early Homo sapiens at Jebel Irhoud

Fossils from Morocco pushed evidence for early Homo sapiens far deeper into Africa’s past.

🎨
c. 100,000–70,000 years agoArt & Ideas

Symbolic Culture at Blombos Cave

Engraved ochre, shell beads, and toolmaking evidence reveal complex symbolic behavior in southern Africa.

🌍
c. 70,000–50,000 years agoHumanity

Major Human Migrations Expand

Homo sapiens populations spread into new regions, interacting with other human groups and adapting to varied environments.

❄️
c. 26,500–19,000 years agoClimate & Environment

Last Glacial Maximum

Ice sheets expanded, sea levels fell, and human communities adapted to colder and drier environments.

Era 02

First Civilizations

Cities, writing, monumental architecture, state power, trade networks, and formal law reshape human communities.
c. 10,000–500 BCE
🌾
c. 10,000 BCE onwardSociety

Agriculture Takes Root

Domestication of plants and animals developed independently in multiple regions and helped support larger settlements.

𒀭
4th millennium BCEScience & Technology

Writing Systems Emerge

Early writing systems developed to record transactions, institutions, stories, and political power.

🔺
c. 26th century BCEPolitics & Society

Pyramids of Giza

Monumental building in Old Kingdom Egypt reflected centralized authority, religious beliefs, labor organization, and engineering.

⚖️
c. 1750 BCEPolitics & Law

Hammurabi’s Judgments

The famous Babylonian stela preserves a large collection of royal legal judgments and principles.

🏙️
c. 3500–3000 BCESociety

Uruk and the Rise of Cities

Large urban centers in Mesopotamia concentrated labor, administration, temples, trade, and political authority.

🧱
c. 2600–1900 BCESociety

Indus Valley Urban Civilization

Harappa and Mohenjo-daro featured planned streets, drainage, craft production, and far-reaching trade.

🌍
c. 2500–1500 BCEPolitics & Society

Kerma and Ancient Nubia

Powerful Nubian states developed along the Nile south of Egypt, controlling trade routes and regional resources.

⚒️
c. 1200–400 BCEScience & Technology

Ironworking in Africa

Iron production developed in parts of Africa and supported new tools, farming systems, crafts, and political growth.

👑
c. 1479–1458 BCEPolitics & Society

Hatshepsut

One of ancient Egypt’s most successful female pharaohs used trade, monumental building, and royal imagery to strengthen her rule.

Era 03

Classical Worlds

Empires, philosophy, citizenship, religious traditions, commerce, and long-distance exchange connect vast regions.
c. 800 BCE–500 CE
🏛️
5th century BCEPolitics & Ideas

Democracy in Athens

Athenian political institutions expanded direct participation for male citizens while excluding many residents.

🪷
1st millennium BCEFaith & Ideas

Philosophies & Faith Traditions

Influential traditions across South Asia, East Asia, the Mediterranean, and Southwest Asia shaped ethics and social life.

🐎
2nd century BCE onwardEconomy & Exchange

Silk Roads Networks

Overland and maritime routes linked merchants, states, travelers, technologies, religions, and disease across Eurasia.

👑
c. 300 BCE–350 CEPolitics & Economy

Kingdom of Kush at Meroë

Kushite rulers governed a major Nile Valley state centered farther south at Meroë.

☸️
c. 3rd century BCE–3rd century CEPolitics

Mauryan Empire and Ashoka

A vast South Asian empire promoted imperial administration and, under Ashoka, inscriptions tied to Buddhist ethics.

🐉
221 BCE onwardPolitics

Imperial China Unifies

Qin unification and the long Han era created durable institutions, infrastructure, and imperial traditions.

⛵
1st–7th centuries CEPolitics & Economy

Kingdom of Aksum

Aksum became a Red Sea power linking the African interior, Arabia, the Mediterranean, and Indian Ocean trade.

🌴
c. 250–900 CESociety & Ideas

Classic Maya Cities

Maya city-states developed monumental architecture, writing, astronomy, calendars, and complex political networks.

📜
551–479 BCEArt & Ideas

Confucius

A Chinese teacher and thinker whose ideas about ethics, education, family, and government shaped East Asian societies for centuries.

☸️
c. 268–232 BCEPolitics & Faith

Ashoka

The Mauryan emperor promoted Buddhist ethics and public welfare after a brutal war of conquest.

🔭
c. 355–415 CEScience & Ideas

Hypatia of Alexandria

A mathematician, astronomer, and philosopher who taught in one of the ancient Mediterranean’s great intellectual centers.

Medieval Worlds

Kingdoms, caliphates, trade cities, universities, migrations, faith, conflict, and cultural exchange transform interconnected regions.
c. 500–1450
👑
c. 500–1500Politics

Kingdoms & Courts

Political authority took many forms, from kingdoms and empires to city-states and decentralized lordships.

📚
8th–13th centuriesScience & Ideas

Centers of Learning Flourish

Scholars across the Islamic world translated, preserved, debated, and extended knowledge in mathematics, medicine, astronomy, and philosophy.

⚔️

Crusading Movements

A series of religiously framed military campaigns transformed politics, trade, and relations across the Mediterranean world.

🦠
14th centuryHumanity

The Black Death

Pandemic plague caused catastrophic mortality across Eurasia and North Africa and reshaped economies and communities.

🐪
c. 700–1200Economy & Exchange

Ghana Empire and Trans-Saharan Trade

West African states grew wealthy by controlling trade in gold, salt, and other goods across the Sahara.

🌊
c. 1000–1500Economy & Exchange

Swahili Coast City-States

African port cities linked the East African coast to Arabia, Persia, India, and the wider Indian Ocean.

🏰
c. 1100–1450Politics & Society

Great Zimbabwe

A major stone-built city became the center of a powerful southern African state tied to regional and Indian Ocean trade.

👑
c. 1235–1600Politics & Economy

Mali Empire

Mali became one of medieval West Africa’s most influential states, controlling gold-producing regions and trade routes.

✨
1324–1325Faith & Economy

Mansa Musa’s Pilgrimage

The ruler of Mali traveled to Mecca with a famous caravan that advertised West Africa’s wealth and connections.

🌌
14th centuryScience & Environment

The 1054 Supernova Remembered

Centuries before the Black Death, skywatchers recorded a brilliant “guest star” later linked to the Crab Nebula.

📚
980–1037Science & Ideas

Ibn Sina (Avicenna)

A Persian polymath whose works on medicine and philosophy influenced scholarship from the Islamic world to Europe.

📖
c. 973–1014Art & Ideas

Murasaki Shikibu

A Japanese court writer associated with The Tale of Genji, often described as one of the world’s earliest great novels.

🦅
1325Politics & Society

Tenochtitlan Is Founded

The Mexica established Tenochtitlan on an island in Lake Texcoco, laying the foundation for one of the largest cities in the Americas.

Early Modern

Print, oceanic travel, conquest, forced migration, religious change, scientific inquiry, and expanding empires reshape the world.
c. 1450–1750
🖨️

Print Expands

Movable-type printing in Europe accelerated the circulation of texts; printing traditions elsewhere had much earlier histories.

⛵
15th–17th centuriesEconomy & Exchange

Oceanic Networks Intensify

European maritime expansion connected existing regional worlds through conquest, commerce, colonization, and forced migration.

Observation, mathematics, instruments, and debate challenged inherited explanations of the natural world.

🎭

👑

A Central African kingdom built regional power and later maintained diplomatic and religious connections with Portugal and the Atlantic world.

🌽
1492 onwardEnvironment & Exchange

Plants, animals, pathogens, and people moved between Afro-Eurasia and the Americas on an unprecedented scale.

⛓️

Millions of Africans were forcibly transported across the Atlantic in one of history’s largest coerced migrations.

⚔️
1591 onwardPolitics & Culture

Songhai Falls, West African Networks Continue

Moroccan forces defeated Songhai at Tondibi, but West African trade, scholarship, and political life continued through new states and networks.

👑
1583–1663Politics & War

Queen Nzinga

The ruler of Ndongo and Matamba used diplomacy, warfare, and shifting alliances to resist Portuguese expansion in Central Africa.

🔭
1564–1642Science & Technology

Galileo Galilei

Telescopic observation and mathematical analysis helped transform astronomy and debates about how knowledge should be tested.

📚
c. 1455Science & Technology

Gutenberg Bible Is Printed

Movable metal type and the printing press helped accelerate the circulation of books in Europe.

Revolutions & Industry

Political revolutions, abolition struggles, nationalism, industrialization, mass migration, and imperial expansion alter societies.
c. 1750–1914
📜
1776Politics & Law

American Independence

The Declaration of Independence articulated claims about rights and government while the Revolutionary War continued.

✊

Haitian Revolution

Enslaved and free people in Saint-Domingue overthrew slavery and colonial rule, creating independent Haiti.

🚂

Industrial Transformation

Steam power, factories, railways, and mechanized production changed work, cities, consumption, and the environment.

⛓️
19th centurySociety & Law

Abolition Expands

Enslaved people, free Black activists, abolitionists, and political movements challenged slavery across the Atlantic world.

⚔️
1760s–1783War & Conflict

American Revolutionary War

Colonial rebellion against Britain became a war for independence with international dimensions.

🇫🇷
1789–1799Revolution

French Revolution

Revolutionary upheaval dismantled old institutions, redefined citizenship, and reshaped European politics.

🌎

Latin American Independence Movements

Revolutions and wars ended Spanish and Portuguese colonial rule across much of the Americas.

🌋

🛡️

A major uprising against East India Company rule transformed British governance in South Asia.

The United States fought a devastating civil war centered on slavery, secession, Union, and emancipation.

🗺️

European powers formalized rules for imperial claims while excluding African representatives from the conference.

🛡️

Ethiopian forces defeated an invading Italian army and preserved Ethiopian sovereignty.

✊
1743–1803Revolution & Society

Toussaint Louverture

A formerly enslaved leader whose political and military leadership was central to the Haitian Revolution.

🗣️
1818–1895Society & Law

Frederick Douglass

After escaping slavery, Douglass became one of the nineteenth century’s most influential abolitionists, writers, and advocates for equal citizenship.

🏯
1868Politics & Society

Meiji Restoration

Political change in Japan ended Tokugawa rule and began a rapid state-led transformation.
