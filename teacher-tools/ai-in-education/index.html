AI in Education

Use generative AI as an assistant for planning, brainstorming, differentiation, feedback, and workflow—not as a substitute for teacher expertise, student thinking, credible sources, or professional responsibility.

Use the Tool, Keep the Judgment

What AI Is Good For—and What Still Needs a Human

Useful Starting Points

  • Brainstorm examples, questions, and lesson structures.
  • Rewrite directions for clarity or accessibility.
  • Generate practice items that a teacher reviews.
  • Create first drafts of rubrics, checklists, or feedback stems.
  • Suggest alternate explanations or scaffolds.

Do Not Outsource

  • Final historical fact-checking or source verification.
  • High-stakes grading without teacher review.
  • Decisions involving student welfare or discipline.
  • Uploading sensitive student information to unapproved systems.
  • Replacing student reasoning with generated answers.

Historical Work Needs Extra Care

AI can confidently invent quotations, citations, dates, archives, interpretations, and source details. Historical claims should be verified against reliable primary sources, reputable scholarship, museums, archives, government collections, or other authoritative references.

Prompt Design

A Better Planning Prompt

Give the role, learners, goal, evidence, constraints, and verification requirement.

Example structure:

“Act as an instructional planning assistant. Help me design a Grade 8 history activity about [topic]. Students should [learning goal]. The evidence of learning will be [product]. Keep the task within [time]. Include supports for [learner need]. Flag any historical claims that require verification and do not invent citations or quotations.”

Academic Integrity

Teach Students to Disclose and Verify

Disclose

Students should know when AI assistance is permitted and how to acknowledge meaningful use when required.

Verify

Generated information should be checked against appropriate sources before being treated as evidence.

Own the Thinking

The final reasoning, interpretation, argument, and decisions should still belong to the learner when those are the objectives being assessed.
