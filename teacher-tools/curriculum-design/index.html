Curriculum & Lesson Design

Start with what learners should understand and be able to do. Then decide what evidence would demonstrate that learning before choosing activities, media, or technology.

A Simple Design Spine

Six Questions Before You Build

01 • PURPOSE

What should students understand?

Clarify the enduring understanding, essential question, standard, or disciplinary practice that matters most.

02 • EVIDENCE

What would count as learning?

Define observable evidence before planning activities. Decide what students will say, make, explain, compare, analyze, or defend.

03 • STARTING POINT

What do learners already know?

Use a brief diagnostic, prompt, quick-write, image, scenario, or retrieval task to surface prior knowledge and misconceptions.

04 • EXPERIENCE

What will students actually do?

Sequence modeling, practice, inquiry, discussion, source work, application, feedback, and reflection around the evidence goal.

05 • ACCESS

What barriers can be reduced?

Provide multiple ways to access information, participate, practice, and show learning without lowering the intellectual target.

06 • REFLECTION

How will the design improve?

Use student work, misconceptions, pacing evidence, and feedback to revise the lesson rather than treating the first version as final.
