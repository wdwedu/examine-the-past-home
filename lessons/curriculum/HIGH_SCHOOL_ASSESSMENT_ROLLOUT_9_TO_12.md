# Examine the Past — High School Assessment Rollout and Readiness Gate
Status: Grade 9 Unit 9.1 pilot committed; Grades 10–12 remain **curriculum content first**. This is the project-wide rollout blueprint, not a claim that 36 assessments are complete.

## Foundation: what remains locked
- The approved Grade 5 Unit 5.1 / Grades 6–8 **visual design** stays the standard: Georgia text; yellow section headings; alternating forest and near-black green question cards; top assessment navigation; 27-point middle-school assessments; white larger response area; blue animated **Check My Writing** trigger; expandable gray rubric directly below the writing box; student copy/print; gated sample after self-review; teacher notes, sources and return links.
- Do not force the same five activities into every unit. Rotate maps, corroboration, evidence sorting, timeline reasoning, source matching, source-type selection, claim/counterclaim, technology-enhanced interactions and original creation.
- Keep self-assessment separate from verified teacher grading. Do not publish unverified self-ratings as state proficiency or an official grade.
- Each final assessment must map questions directly to the specific lessons and current applicable state indicators/assessment limits.

## Grade progression
| Grade | Source expectations | Written/performance demand | Assessment style |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 U.S. History | At least two independently traceable primary sources plus a secondary context; 150–300 word document passages where appropriate | Qualified historical thesis, corroboration, counterclaim, contextualization, generally 3–5 developed paragraphs when appropriate | Document-based items, causal inference, historical argument and evidence selection |
| 10 Civics & Government | Founding texts, constitutional provisions, selected court opinions, policy data and scenarios | Constitutional argument, civic decisions, multiple perspectives, policy recommendation | Released-government-style source sets, scenario judgment, case study and informed civic action |
| 11 Modern World History | Multiple perspectives across regions; translated and dated original texts, maps, tables and historical interpretations | Comparative evidence-based essay, causation, continuity/change, counterargument | Multi-source DBQs, comparative maps and causal-network explanations |
| 12 Social Studies Electives | Student-located primary and secondary sources with independent validation; methodological critique | Research question and literature review, research portfolio, original exhibit/presentation or project | Capstone, historiography, policy analysis, peer review, research defense |

High-school assessments are not required to keep the middle-school 27-point allocation. Decide points based on the actual task, while keeping common visual and accessible interaction patterns and clear scoring.

- Four lesson topics checked: Reconstruction's Unfinished Legacy; Rise of Big Business; Labor, Immigration and Urban America; Gilded Age Wealth and Inequality.
- Assessment route: `lessons/curriculum/grade-9-united-states-history/unit-9-1-assessment/`
- Authenticated source links: National Archives Fourteenth Amendment, National Archives Sherman Antitrust Act, LOC 1890 population-density map; NPS Pullman Strike history as **secondary** interpretation.
- Two brief original document-language extracts, and labeled educational summaries.
- Six original document-reasoning multiple-choice items with plausible alternatives, three document matching questions, inquiry order, evidence-claim checks, qualified writing task and optional curator exhibit.

This pilot serves as high-school bridge review, NOT proof that the Progressivism curriculum has been assessed. The separate Grade 8 bridge source lab is under `grade-8-early-united-states-history/industrial-urban-bridge-1877-1890/`.
- Pilot still requires deployed browser checks, authentic document review and teacher-led scoring calibration before classroom adoption.

## Mandatory release sequence
1. Repair remaining middle-school assessment quality concerns: false easy distractors, limited source authenticity, complete 2025 state indicator crosswalk, real-browser E2E scoring / keyboard / clipboard / printing / mobile QA, teacher grading workflow. Current grades 6–8 are repository-complete but NOT certified for summative use.
2. Verify Grade 9.1 in a real browser at actual deployed URL; confirm authored sources and correct answer keys; pilot with student/teacher feedback.
3. Develop Grade 9.2–9.11 using substantive existing Grade 9 lessons. Give every item a source register, grade-level readability/rhetorical-complexity review, answer rationale and state-objective mapping. Do not simply paste old lesson knowledge checks unchanged.
4. For Grade 10 (9 units / 31 lessons), write full source-supported lessons before summative tests; use Maryland's June 2025 Government Framework and released assessment limits, explicitly evaluate legal and civic reasoning. https://msde.maryland.gov/media/17919 and https://assessments.msde.maryland.gov/public/assessment-limit-examples-govt
5. For Grade 11 (11 units / 40 lessons), develop and verify substantive modern-world lessons before writing document-based assessments; crosswalk to the 2025 Modern World History Framework; diversify regions and perspectives.
6. For Grade 12 (5 units / 20 lessons), develop research/historiography/economics/government/geography elective tasks, student portfolios and teacher-reviewed authentic products; avoid a single uniform multiple-choice final.
7. For every unit: confirm that all lessons exist and teach the assessed skills, factual details are independently supported, source creators/dates/licenses are known, scoring max/min and partial/missing handling work, rubric placement is preserved, and deployed desktop/mobile tests pass.

## Release acceptance criteria
- Content accuracy and attributions: reviewed with credible, original, contextualized and respectful source materials (including community-informed sources for Indigenous history).
- Grade-appropriate readability: measured lexical complexity, vocabulary supports, interpretation depth, writing expectations; not only word count.
- Item validity: answer indices and feedback independently checked, plausible distractors, sources genuinely required for response, no obvious 'everyone/always' gimmicks.
- Actual historical inquiry: questions, tools, evidence, corroboration, argument, communication and creation.
- Teacher assessment: clear separate scoring for auto-check and reviewed writing; collaboration not conflated with individual score.
- Browser verification: Chrome desktop/mobile, focus, screenreader semantics, touch, print/copy, modal, reset, grade totals, sample gate and real URLs.
- Release signoff: independent teacher/historian and functional reviewer, then lock exact version/commit.
