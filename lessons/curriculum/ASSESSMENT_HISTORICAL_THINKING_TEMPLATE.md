# Examine the Past — Historical Thinking Assessment Master Template (Grades 5–8)

Status: Pilot design derived from Grade 5 Unit 5.1. Apply after piloting and QA; do not blindly duplicate questions or assessment activity formats.

## Guiding outcome
Students should leave each unit knowing more history and being better at investigating a question, evaluating information, defending a conclusion, and creating something meaningful from evidence.

## Non-negotiable design
- Preserve site-wide Georgia type, yellow section headings, alternate normal and nearly black forest-green question cards, clear white numbered prompts.
- Retain top navigation, expectation card, accessible scoring and keyboard interaction.
- Keep readable, responsive writing box (taller than previous 160px).
- Keep original rubric directly below writing box. Collapsible gray matrix remains unchanged in position, rating mechanics, four criteria, and 0–3 rating scale.
- Use blue "Check My Writing" trigger with subtle shimmer/bounce, with a reduced-motion fallback.
- Self-entered writing points are labeled SELF-REVIEWED, not objectively graded.
- Sample answer revealed only after the student finishes and grades, with a second deliberate click to show it; samples are teaching examples, not answers to copy.
- Keep optional creation/collaboration extension separate from the individual unit score unless explicitly redesigning scoring.

## Pilot scoring (Unit 5.1)
| Part | Points | Required cognitive work |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Multiple choice (6 items) | 6 | Apply, compare, evaluate source evidence |
| Source investigation (3 matches) | 3 | Select appropriate evidence for questions |
| Build an investigation (3 ordered moves) | 3 | Ask, investigate, defend |
| Defend a conclusion (3 judgments) | 3 | Identify supported / unsupported claims |
| Constructed response | 12 | Compare, support, reason, consider limitations |
| **Total** | **27** | |

Other units may use different activity mechanics and a different point blueprint, but must clearly disclose scoring. Do not force the exact same activities into every unit.

## Evidence packet quality
- Use 2–4 concise sources for Grade 5, more and longer sources as appropriate by grade; include maps, visuals, artifacts, oral history, primary or secondary excerpts when available.
- Cite original author/creator, date, provenance, permission and interpretive context when using authentic artifacts or sources.
- Mark fictional teaching examples as fictional and original summaries as instructional paraphrases. Never label them primary-source quotations.
- Where feasible, use nation-informed resources and specific tribal/national names rather than flattening all Indigenous histories into one culture.
- A correct answer must be demonstrably supported; distractors should be plausible and reveal common misconceptions, not silly or insulting.
- Do not tell the student the correct answers before submission; give explanations after grading.
- Check source accessibility (alt text / transcript) and mobile readability.

## Skills tagging for every item
Assign at least one of:
1. Historical context and content
2. Asking focused questions
3. Source evaluation and corroboration
4. Evidence-based reasoning and argument
5. Communication and historical explanation
6. Creation / transfer to a new problem
Track points by skill internally so future adaptive practice can target gaps; never infer a fully mastered skill from one easy question.

## Grade progression (working design targets, verify with applicable state and local standards)
- Grade 5: short accessible passages and sources; meaningful distractors; 2–3 developed paragraphs for this comparison task; no universal five-paragraph requirement.
- Grade 6: longer and more varied sources, source limitations, causal comparisons, more independent explanations.
- Grade 7: conflicting perspectives, corroboration, inquiry choices, extended multi-source responses.
- Grade 8: richer primary and secondary source sets, multi-part document-based questions, strong claim-evidence-reasoning and counterargument when appropriate.
- High school: source-heavy examinations and sustained essays/DBQs adjusted to course and state exam requirements.
Grade-level expectations and actual readability should be checked for every unit; do not rely on word count alone.

## Authentic creation & collaboration
An independent investigation or museum-curator task can follow the quiz with product choices (exhibit panel, mini-documentary, slides, annotated map, audio explanation). Include a research question, at least two evidence types, conclusion, and limitation. Teachers may optionally assign teams, but assess each member's contribution, evidence checking, and communication separately. Provide a simple teacher rubric rather than an unverified auto-generated collaboration grade.

## Quality assurance gate before applying to the next unit
1. Verify history with credible sources, original documents if practical, and nation-informed perspectives as appropriate.
2. Check all six multiple-choice correct answer indices against questions and feedback.
3. Verify the answer key for matching, ordering, supported-evidence selections, total points, unanswered behavior and full reset.
4. Confirm grading feedback is shown only at the appropriate moment.
5. Verify typing edits invalidate prior writing self-ratings; completed sample view is locked again when the student revises.
6. Check rubric remains below the writing area, expands correctly, and does not shift to side-by-side.
7. Test keyboard, touch and desktop interactions, print/copy, modal focus, reduce-motion, and mobile overflow.
8. Label writing scores accurately and offer a teacher review path.
9. Verify headings, navigation, cards, font, contrast and unique questions.
10. Confirm actual deployed site matches the reviewed source commit before considering the unit approved.

## Activity rotation suggestions
- Map interpretation and annotated location claims
- Chronological reconstruction / sequencing with a justification
- Source investigation and corroboration
- Competing claims / evidence sort
- Cause and consequence chain
- Continuity and change from visual data
- Perspective analysis with source limitations
- Historical question design and research planning
- Museum-curator exhibit or evidence-based creation extension
