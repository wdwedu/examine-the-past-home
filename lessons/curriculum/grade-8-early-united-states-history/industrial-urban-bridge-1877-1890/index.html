GRADE 8 · HISTORICAL BRIDGE · 1877–1890

Industrial and Urban America

Historical Source Investigation · A bridge from Reconstruction to modern U.S. history

This is a separate evidence-based practice lab. It does not change the approved Unit 8.8 assessment or its 27-point scoring.

Investigation Question

Did industrial growth improve opportunities equally for all Americans between 1877 and 1890?

Examine three different kinds of historical records. Use evidence to explain how industry and government action shaped opportunities, and what each source cannot tell you.

Explore the Evidence

Source A · Sherman Antitrust Act (1890)

Creator: U.S. Congress · Type: Federal law · Date: July 2, 1890

This federal act addressed monopolization and certain restraints on interstate trade. Its passage is evidence of congressional concern about corporate practices, but it does not by itself prove how effective enforcement was.

Examine the law at the National Archives

Summary is instructional paraphrase, not a direct quotation

Source B · Chinese Exclusion Act (1882)

Creator: U.S. Congress · Type: Federal law · Date: May 6, 1882

The act barred most immigration of Chinese laborers for a decade. It provides direct evidence of an exclusionary federal policy, but its words alone do not establish every immigrant family's experience.

Examine the law at the National Archives

Summary is instructional paraphrase, not a direct quotation

Source C · U.S. Population-Density Map (1890)

Creator: Rand, McNally & Co. · Type: Census-based historical map · Date: Published 1892, depicting 1890 census data

The map represents population distribution at the end of the decade. It may show where population was concentrated; it cannot directly establish wages, individual motives for migration, or the political rights of residents.

Inspect the original map at the Library of Congress

Read the map's legend and category definitions

Source D · Industrialization in Context

Type: Historical inquiry guidance · Reference: Maryland State Department of Education, 2025 framework

To measure industrial growth fairly, historians should distinguish economic output from its unequal effects on workers, immigrants, farmers, and communities. Compare technology and transport with labor conditions and government decisions.

See Maryland's U.S. History Bridge Framework

Secondary instructional guidance; not a primary record

Show What You Know

1. Which source can directly establish that Congress restricted Chinese labor immigration in 1882?

2. Which extra source best tests whether the Sherman Antitrust Act changed company practices?

3. Which claim is supported by the 1890 map alone?

Historical Performance Task

Write 3–4 well-developed paragraphs. Make a claim about whether industrial growth improved opportunities equally. Use at least two types of evidence, including one of the actual primary records above. Compare government actions and economic changes, explain one limitation of the evidence, and consider a reasonable alternative interpretation.

You may prepare your draft in another document and paste it here. A teacher should review your argument. This lab is not part of the 27-point Unit 8.8 assessment.
