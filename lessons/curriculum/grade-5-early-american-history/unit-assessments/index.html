GRADE 5 · UNIT 5.1 · CUMULATIVE CHECK

Indigenous North America

End-of-unit knowledge and evidence assessment. Six multiple-choice questions, source matching, an inquiry sequence, an evidence-selection task, and a written explanation assess the unit objectives.

Complete after Lessons 5.1.1–5.1.3. No student response is transmitted or stored on a server.

Assessment expectations

27 possible points: multiple choice (6), source matching (3), inquiry sequence (3), evidence selection (3), and writing (12). Suggested proficiency: 80%. There is no timer, no account and no automatic submission to a teacher.

Focus: regional diversity, geography, governance, historical evidence, primary-source limits and Indigenous continuity. Writing receives a provisional checklist-based estimate. Review the suggested rubric ratings: automated checks cannot verify historical accuracy or the quality of cited evidence.

7. Match Sources to Questions · 3 points

Select the best source for each question. Each correct match earns 1 point.

8. Put an Inquiry in Order · 3 points

Assign a different position to each step: 1 is first, 3 is last. Each correct position earns 1 point.

9. Select Supported Conclusions · 3 points

Evidence: The Eastern Woodlands reading describes rivers, agriculture, and Haudenosaunee longhouses. The Southwest reading describes Ancestral Pueblo agriculture and water management. These are regional examples, not descriptions of every nation.

Select all conclusions supported by this evidence. Earn 1 point for each supported conclusion selected and each unsupported conclusion correctly left unselected. Leaving everything unchecked counts as unanswered.

Written Performance Task · 12 points

Compare how two Indigenous societies or regions developed practices suited to their homelands. Identify at least two kinds of evidence and explain one limitation of the evidence. Do not generalize about all Indigenous nations.

Interactive Writing Rubric · Four criteria, 0–3 points each

Select a level for each criterion to see what it means. Writing estimates are suggestions; a teacher can change each rating after reviewing the response.

Reading the score

The first four formats provide 15 automatically checked points. Writing contributes up to 12 points through four separate rubric ratings. A score of at least 22/27 reaches 80%; the overall result remains provisional until writing is reviewed.

Standards: thematic alignment with Maryland social studies Standards 2, 3, 5 and 6. Specific district indicator alignment should be confirmed before formal use. Not an official Maryland or psychometrically validated test.

Adjustments: read questions aloud, allow additional time, provide a print transcript or score the written response orally using the same rubric. Maintain the outcome, not one mandated response medium.
