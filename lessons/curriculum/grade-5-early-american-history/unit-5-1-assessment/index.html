GRADE 5 · UNIT 5.1 · CUMULATIVE CHECK

Indigenous North America

End-of-unit knowledge and evidence assessment. Ten automatically graded questions move from recall through interpretation and source evaluation, followed by a teacher-scored historical argument.

Complete after Lessons 5.1.1–5.1.3. Because the other two lessons are still in development, this assessment can currently serve as a diagnostic model. No student response is transmitted or stored on a server.

Assessment expectations

Choose one answer per question. 10 questions · 10 points. Suggested proficiency: 8/10, with additional written argument review. There is no timer, no account and no automatic submission to a teacher.

Focus: regional diversity, geography, governance, historical evidence, primary-source limits and Indigenous continuity. The writing task is not machine-graded because historical arguments require human review.

Written performance task · Teacher review

Compare how two Indigenous societies or regions developed practices suited to their homelands. Identify at least two kinds of evidence and explain one limitation of the evidence. Do not generalize about all Indigenous nations.

Show writing rubric

Claim accuracy (0–3), relevant evidence (0–3), clear reasoning (0–3), recognition of source limits and diversity (0–3). Suggested written proficiency: 9/12. A teacher or reviewer scores the open response independently.

Teacher notes and alignment

Assessment blueprint: Q1–3 recognize diversity, geography and resources; Q4–6 interpret governance, community accounts and material sources; Q7–10 compare explanations, evaluate sourcing and correct misconceptions. The separate written task tests synthesis and argumentation.

Standards: thematic alignment with Maryland social studies Standards 2, 3, 5 and 6. Specific district indicator alignment should be confirmed before formal use. Not an official Maryland or psychometrically validated test.

Adjustments: read questions aloud, allow additional time, provide a print transcript or score the written response orally using the same rubric. Maintain the outcome, not one mandated response medium.
