How Do Historians Study Religion?

Learn a respectful method that distinguishes belief claims from historical evidence while taking both seriously as sources.

World ReligionsSkill LabGrades 7–12 / Adult45–60 min
1 · Launch

Start With the Question

Essential Question: How can we study religion academically without trying to prove or disprove a faith tradition?

Religious traditions make claims about meaning, the sacred, community, morality, and human experience. Historians ask different questions: what people believed, how traditions changed, what sources show, and how religion interacted with society.

Warm-up prompt

What is the difference between explaining what a tradition teaches and deciding whether that teaching is true?

4 · Build Context

Historical Background

Academic study of religion uses methods from history, anthropology, archaeology, literature, sociology, and related fields. A historian can accurately describe a sacred narrative while separately asking what external evidence can establish about its historical development.

Context connection

Respect is not the same as avoiding analysis. Strong inquiry can be both careful and intellectually rigorous.

6 · Explore Evidence

Evidence Lab

Two-Lens Source Method
The finished activity will pair a religious source with contextual historical evidence and explicitly label the two lenses.

Observe → Infer → Question

Observe: What does the source say or depict?
Infer: What can it reveal about the community that used it?
Question: Which claims require additional historical evidence?
Historian's source check

Who created the evidence? When and where? For what audience or purpose? What does it reveal, and what can it not prove by itself?
