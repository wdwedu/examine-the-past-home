# Grade 9 Units 9.5–9.6 — Academic Source and Teacher-Voice Audit
Date: November 23, 2026
Status: Repository content revision committed; not independently approved for classroom release.

## Scope
Eight lessons, four in Unit 9.5 and four in Unit 9.6. The existing three reading panels and eight-section Grade 5-matched lesson presentation were preserved.

## Changes
- Rewrote all 24 reading panels to remove formulaic prompts and strengthen precise, readable historical storytelling.
- Supplied 16 source records (14 unique URLs). Each has a title, creator/provenance, date or contextual dating, original link, type and a caution about what the evidence can prove.
- Replaced 24 multiple-choice questions and 24 investigative tasks with lesson-specific evidence questions.
- Source categories distinguish original letters, photographs, amendments, statutes and advertisements from Federal Reserve and Department of Labor retrospective research; collection/guide pages are explicitly labeled as such.
- Updated lesson and assessment cache references without redesigning navigation.

## Lesson-specific evidence
1. Changing American Culture — LOC Scopes-trial photograph and April 1927 automobile advertisement. Images reveal a documented moment or advertising strategy, not nationwide consensus.
2. Harlem Renaissance — LOC original October 29, 1925 Langston Hughes-to-Walter White letter and NAACP primary-source exhibition. The second entry is a collection; teachers must choose a particular dated second item before treating the comparison as complete.
3. Prohibition — eighteenth and twenty-first amendments via National Archives constitutional transcription. The Eighteenth Amendment does not itself ban consumption. Both citations use the same transcript URL but different amendments.
4. Consumer Culture — dated 1927 Renault automobile advertisement and a LOC cataloged contemporary National Automobile Chamber of Commerce publication (choose exact dated edition before using numeric claims).
5. Market Crash and Economic Crisis — separate Federal Reserve History analyses of 1929 crash and 1930–31 banking panics. **Both are secondary analysis**; original market/bank datasets still needed for full claim-level primary corroboration.
6. Living Through the Great Depression — LOC 1936 Lange Migrant Mother image and comparison guide to original series. The modern guide is context; the photographs are primary evidence. Photographic representativeness and Thompson's own voice remain limitations.
7. Roosevelt's New Deal — National Archives Social Security Act and FDR Presidential Library March 12, 1933 banking radio address via an institutional education page. The speech and statute represent different purposes and years.
8. New Deal Transformation — National Archives Wagner Act and Department of Labor historical account of FLSA. Historical account is **secondary**; it is not a facsimile of the 1938 enrolled statute.

- All 24 questions: three different alternatives; numeric answer key in range.
- No prior boilerplate markers in revised readings or questions.
- Grade 9 total retained at 45 lessons; 15 still have no source records, and 22 still have formulaic legacy assessment prompts.
- Lesson-loader and assessment HTML cache references updated; engine and loader parsed successfully.

## Academic review blockers
- Many source links are official catalog items, exhibition selections or institutional teaching hubs; named artifacts are grounded, but not all original documents have exact page numbers or excerpts that students can access directly.
- Period publication edition year and quantitative denominators must be established before presenting statistics.
- The bank-crash comparison currently has **secondary** sources only. A primary evidence item from 1929–31 must be attached before it meets the strict primary-record release target.
- The Harlem Renaissance second record is a collection, not a second selected item. Editorial work is still required for source-level choice.
- Teacher review of every historical assertion, copyright/permissions for displayed artifacts, accessibility, live link behavior and live assessment/browser QA remain outstanding.
