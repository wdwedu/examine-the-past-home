## Sources and academic safeguards
### Unit 9.3
1. Why Did America Become an Overseas Power?: U.S. Teller Amendment, a Philippine declaration of independence in the Philippine National Commission for Culture and the Arts sourcebook, and José Martí/Máximo Gómez's 1895 Montecristi Manifesto (Dominican archival catalog). These establish distinctly U.S., Filipino and Cuban political voices; NCCA translation and catalog metadata must not be misrepresented as original manuscript transcription.
2. Spanish–American War: original U.S.–Spain Treaty of Paris (FRUS 1898 d712a), Filipino independence declaration, U.S. Teller Amendment and previously attached McKinley/war-declaration records. The treaty *relinquishes* Spain's sovereignty over Cuba but *cedes* Puerto Rico, Guam and the Philippines; a diplomatic agreement among the signatories is not proof of consent by local populations.
3. Changing Foreign Policy: John Hay Open Door series, a **secondary** State Department institutional chronology; Theodore Roosevelt's **December 1904** annual message in the Smithsonian exhibition; and National Archives framing of the Roosevelt Corollary. Do not confuse a 1904 speech with a later exhibit heading or a secondary synopsis with the signed diplomatic note.

### Unit 9.4
4. Causes of WWI: dated Austrian ultimatum (July 23, 1914), Library of Congress contemporary-document compilation and Austrian National Library contextual exhibition. A source's allegation is not itself confirmation of guilt. Distinguish assassination, ultimatum, and broader state decisions in the July Crisis.
5. U.S. Entry into WWI: January 1917 Zimmermann Telegram, Wilson's April 2 declaration-of-war request, National Archives April 6 congressional resolution collection pointer. Explicitly state the telegram **proposed** an alliance; it does not establish Mexico accepted.
6. Americans on the Home Front: Library of Congress catalog for James Montgomery Flagg's 1917 recruitment poster; National Archives DocsTeach record for the June 15, 1917 Espionage Act. Distinguish poster persuasion, statute wording and lived civilian experiences; acknowledge that the later 1918 Sedition Act is a separate law.
7. Treaty of Versailles: Yale Avalon original Part I League covenant, Wilson's National Archives Fourteen Points, and official U.S. Senate historical account. The 1918 peace proposal is not the 1919 treaty. Senate refusal explains U.S. nonmembership; one treaty does not by itself establish inevitability of WWII.

## Limitations requiring further checking
- The Declaration of Philippine Independence is a translated primary document inside a later government sourcebook; record the translation provenance and assign a precise page range when excerpting.
- The Montecristi Manifesto link is a catalog record rather than a freely accessible complete textual facsimile. Provide a checked accessible transcription or classroom excerpt before representing it as student-readable.
- U.S. congressional resolution references and several other collection pages require an exact archival item link before claiming fully item-level access.
- No claim of full academic 'error-proof' certification or human expert signoff is made.
