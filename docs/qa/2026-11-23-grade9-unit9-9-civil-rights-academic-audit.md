## Sources and cautions
1. **Segregation and the Fight for Equality:** *Plessy v. Ferguson* majority opinion and Justice Harlan dissent, National Archives (May 18, 1896); LOC NAACP original-object exhibition. The latter is an **archival collection**; choose a specific dated item before claiming two item-level primary documents.
2. **Brown v. Board:** Supreme Court opinion May 17, 1954 and Eisenhower Executive Order 10730 September 23, 1957, National Archives. Ruling and implementation are distinct.
3. **King and Nonviolent Resistance:** Police report of Rosa Parks's arrest, December 1955, NARA DocsTeach; King, *Letter from Birmingham Jail*, April 16, 1963, Stanford King Institute. A police report cannot explain Parks's long activism; King's letter gives an activist's argument to a specific audience. Stanford explicitly notes reproduction permission limitations. **Link, do not reproduce copyrighted long passages or embedded media without authorization.**
4. **Malcolm X:** Original 1964 *Ballot or the Bullet* working notes, NYPL, and dated speech text presented by National Constitution Center. Notes and a later edited transcript are different source formats. His political outlook evolved; do not oversimplify his contrast with King. Verify transcript edition against the delivered speech before quoting.
5. **Movement's Lasting Impact:** Civil Rights Act of July 2, 1964 and Voting Rights Act of August 6, 1965, National Archives. Different scopes; neither text establishes actual local compliance by itself.
